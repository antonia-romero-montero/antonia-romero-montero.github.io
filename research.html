The processing of sexual violence complaints against adolescents in Chile: evidence on archiving, media salience, and political-territorial context (2009 to 2019)

Master's thesis, Universidad Alberto Hurtado. Approved. Advisor: Bárbara Boggiano.

Using administrative records from Chile's public prosecution service for 2009 to 2019, I study how complaints of sexual violence involving women aged 14 to 18 are processed. Most complaints are archived, and the archiving rate is high and similar across prosecutors. I then ask whether media attention changes outcomes: stacked event studies show that it increases the number of complaints filed without changing archiving decisions. A final exploratory part relates judicial behavior to legislative voting and social mobilization.

Methods: stacked event studies (PPML), leave-one-out estimators, wild cluster bootstrap.

Emerging market business cycles: a replication for Chile

Course project, Macroeconomics II, Universidad Alberto Hurtado.

This project replicates Aguiar and Gopinath (2007) and extends their analysis by adding Chile, using data from the Banco Central de Chile. The project compares HP-filtered business cycle moments with those of the countries in the original paper, estimates the structural parameters of the model, and studies the implied impulse responses.

Tools: R, Matlab, LaTeX.
