I study how Chile's criminal justice system responds to sexual violence against adolescent women.

I'm an economist finishing my MSc in Economics at Universidad Alberto Hurtado (UAH) in Santiago, with an approved thesis in applied microeconometrics. My work uses administrative microdata and quasi-experimental designs to understand how public institutions handle crime, and how media attention and political context shape what they do.

I work mainly in applied microeconomics and economic development, with a particular interest in education, crime and gender. I also follow macroeconomics closely. At UAH I am a research assistant and a teaching assistant for courses in macroeconomics, microeconomics and econometrics. I completed my degree in Ingeniería Comercial at UAH, ranked first in my class and graduating cum laude.

Research interests

  • Crime and criminal justice
  • Gender-based violence
  • Economic development, education and gender
  • Applied microeconomics and causal inference
